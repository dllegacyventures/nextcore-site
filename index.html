02 / Systems

What we actually build

Six systems we build most often. Every one of them replaces work somebody is doing by hand today — not work somebody might do one day.

  1. 01

    Enquiry & qualification agent

    Every inbound enquiry — form, inbox, DM — gets read, classified against your own fit criteria, written into the CRM, answered, and put on a follow-up date. The enquiry that used to wait until nine the next morning is handled at three in the night.

    Runs on web form · inbox · CRM

  2. 02

    Customer support agent

    Answers the questions your team answers a hundred times a month, out of your own documents and resolved tickets. Anything outside its brief is escalated to a person with the context already assembled. It is built to say “I don’t know” rather than to improvise.

    Runs on helpdesk · knowledge base · inbox

  3. 03

    Content production line

    One source — a recorded call, a document, a briefing — becomes the formats you actually publish: posts, newsletters, clips, product copy. Output is draft quality by design; a person still approves before anything goes out.

    Runs on transcripts · documents · publishing tools

  4. 04

    Document generation

    Offers, contracts, reports and handover summaries assembled from your data and your templates, worded the same way every time. It removes the copy-and-paste step, which is where the wrong client name and the old price come from.

    Runs on templates · CRM · document storage

  5. 05

    Research & reporting

    Standing questions answered on a schedule: what moved in these accounts, what did competitors publish, what do this week’s numbers say. Delivered as a short written briefing to the people who decide — not as another dashboard nobody opens.

    Runs on public sources · internal data · email

  6. 06

    Internal workflow orchestration

    The handovers between tools and people: onboarding a client, closing a month, routing an approval, chasing the missing document. These are the steps that get forgotten, because they live in one person’s head and that person was on holiday.

    Runs on the stack you already pay for

If your process is not on this list, that is not a problem — it is the normal case. The assessment exists to find out which of your work fits and which does not.

05 / FAQ

Before you ask

The eight questions that come up in almost every first conversation, answered here so the call can start further along.

What does it cost?

The assessment is a fixed fee agreed before it starts. The build is quoted after the assessment, against a scope written down in advance. Operation runs as a monthly retainer. Everything is quoted in writing before any work begins, and there is no usage-based surprise from our side. Model and infrastructure costs are either billed at cost or paid by you directly to the provider, so you can see them.

How long until something actually runs?

The assessment usually takes about a week. A first working system typically follows two to four weeks after that. We deliberately cut the first build small enough to reach production, because a system in use teaches you more in a fortnight than a specification does in a quarter.

What do you need from us?

Access to the systems the automation touches, one person who can answer questions about how the process really works, and one person who can decide. Plus examples: the documents, the messages, the edge cases. The single largest cause of a slow build is not technology, it is waiting for access.

What happens to our data?

It stays in your systems wherever the design allows. Where a model has to see it, we tell you which provider, under what terms, in which region it is processed, and whether the provider trains on it — we select providers where it does not. We do not sell client data and we do not use it to train public models. The details are in the privacy policy.

What if the model or one of our tools changes?

That is exactly what the retainer is for. Models are deprecated, interfaces change, prices change, and your own business rules change. An unmaintained automation does not stop with an error message — it keeps running and gets quietly worse, which is the expensive failure mode.

Are we locked in?

No. The systems run in your accounts on your credentials. At the end of a build you can take everything over; handover documentation is part of delivery, not an extra. If you leave, you leave with the systems.

Do you guarantee results?

No. We agree up front which numbers we are measured against and report weekly against them. Anyone who guarantees an outcome for a system that talks to your customers is selling you something other than engineering.

Do you work with private individuals?

No. NextCore serves businesses only. We do not sell courses, memberships or consumer products, and we do not take on private clients.
